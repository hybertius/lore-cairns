# On Temporal Adjacency

Claude wasn't hallucinating.

Claude wasn't wrong.

Claude was being aggressively helpful.

🧐: "Can you explain var vs let?"

Claude: *"Certainly. Also here is a future bug, an asynchronous execution model, closure behaviour, hoisting, browser globals, a historical overview of ECMAScript, and a small glimpse into your destiny."*

The educational equivalent of:

🧐: "I asked for directions to the bakery."

and receiving:

Claude: "Here is a topographical survey of the entire continent."

What makes it funny is that the example is actually excellent.

*It's just temporally misplaced.*

Claude: "Here's the answer.

Here's the next answer.

Here's the answer you'll need next Tuesday.

Here's the answer to the question you'll ask after discovering the previous answer."

The issue was never:

The information is wrong.

Instead:

The traveller is still standing here.

"Delivery optimisation != Useful for receiver"

Not:

Claude.

But:

a recurring optimisation tendency.

Not:

the deliverer.

But:

the delivery strategy.
